TIME

  Terrestrial Time ("TT") output was requested. This continuous timescale
is the relativistic proper time of a clock at rest on the Earth's geoid (at
"mean sea level"), but defined for a specified gravitational time dilation,
so a coordinate time. It is appropriate for observations and measurements
from the surface of the Earth.

  TT runs at a uniform rate of one SI second per second and is independent of
irregularities in Earth's rotation. It is a theoretical ideal, approximated
(with a constant offset of 32.184 seconds) by the measured International Atomic
Time (TAI) after 1955.  TT is the relativistic successor to the now deprecated
non-relativistic Ephemeris Time (ET).

  The TT scale requested here is a time-scale conversion from internal
Barycentric Dynamical Time (TDB) and differs periodically from TDB with an
amplitude less than 2 ms for several thousand years centered on present.
The time-scale conversion for Earth is used regardless of observer location
within the solar system, although clock rates may differ due to the local
gravity field.

REFERENCE FRAME AND COORDINATES

  International Celestial Reference Frame (ICRF)
